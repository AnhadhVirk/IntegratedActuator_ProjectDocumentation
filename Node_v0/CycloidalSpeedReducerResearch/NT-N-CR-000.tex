\section{Problem Framing}
\begin{researchquestion}[System Direction]
  What cycloidal speed reducer architecture maximises efficiency (W/W \%), minimises backlash (arcmin) and ensures durable and long-lasting operation (cycles/failure) under the specified load and speed conditions?
\end{researchquestion}

\section{Preliminary Direction}
This research document will collate results from multiple research papers and notes to
inform the design of a cycloidal speed reducer for Node v0.
The goal is to identify the most promising cycloidal speed reducer architecture that
meets the specified design goals (NT-N-G1, NT-N-G7).
The motor housing architecture has been designed and the first iteration will inform
the cycloidal speed reducer design.
The motor housing output interface is the only interface that the cycloidal speed
reducer will need to integrate with, and therefore the cycloidal speed reducer design
will not be overly constrained by the motor housing architecture.

\section{Assumptions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Cycloidal speed reducers are the most suitable architecture for Node v0, due to
         their high torque density, low backlash, and compact form factor. Another
        major factor is the ease of cycloidal manufacturing, due to its simple geometry
         and the ability to use standard manufacturing processes.
  \item Steel is the most suitable material for the cycloidal disks and roller pins due
         to its high strength and wear resistance, and aluminium is suitable for the
        other components, due to its machinability.
\end{itemize}

\section{Cycloidal Speed Reducer Operating Principles}
To understand cycloidal speed reducers, a brief introduction into cycloidal curves and
their geometries is required.
A cycloidal curve is created by tracing a point on a circle, as it rolls without
slipping along a line.
This is demonstrated in the following figure:
\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{figures/CycloidalCurveCreation.png}
  \caption{Cycloidal curve generation}
  \label{fig:cycloidal_curve}
\end{figure}

When a cycloidal curve is generated with the base being another circle, it generates
either an epicycloidal curve or a hypocycloidal curve.
In the epicycloidal case, the curve is produced by tracing a cycloidal curve on the
outside of a circle, while a hypocycloidal curve is produced by tracing a cycloidal
curve on the inside of a circle.
The following 2 figures show a epicycloidal and hypocycloidal curve, respectively:
\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{figures/EpicycloidalCurve.png}
  \caption{Epicycloidal curve generation}
  \label{fig:epicycloidal_curve}
\end{figure}
\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{figures/HypocycloidalCurve.jpg}
  \caption{Hypocycloidal curve generation}
  \label{fig:hypocycloidal_curve}
\end{figure}

A cycloidal curve is an easy to understand case of a trochoidal curve. A trochoidal
curve is the same as the cycloidal curve, except that the tracing point is not on the
surface of the rolling circle, but is instead offset from the surface of the rolling
circle by a distance $r$.
A hypotrochoidal curve and epitrochoidal curve are the trochoidal versions of
hypocycloidal and epicycloidal curves.
This is shown in the following figure (note that a prolate curve is where the $r$ is
greater than the tracing circle radius, while a curtate is where $r$ is less than the
tracing circle radius):
\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{figures/ProlateCurtateTrochoidCurves.jpg}
  \caption{Prolate and Curtate Trochoidal curves}
  \label{fig:trochoidal_curves}
\end{figure}
\newpage
Hypotrochoidal and epitrochoidal curves have a number of interesting properties:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The number of lobes, or segments, in the trochoid is the exact same as the
         ratio $k = \frac{R}{r}$, as labeled above in \ref{fig:epicycloidal_curve} and
        \ref{fig:hypocycloidal_curve}.
  \item The number of rotations of the tracing circle about its center for one full
         rotation around the base circle is $k + 1$ for epitrochoidal curves and $k -
        1$  for hypotrochoidal curves.
\end{itemize}

The second property can be used to reduce speed and convert to torque.
The kinematic relationship that is used commonly is the number of revolutions done by
the tracing circle to roll one full revolution around the base circle.
This is derived by Santos-Pereira in \cite{CycloidalGeometry}, and shows that for the
epicycloid/epitrochoid case, the number is:
\[
  \frac{D}{d}+1
\]
and for the hypocycloid/hypotrochoid case:
\[
  \frac{D}{d}-1
\]
where $D$ is the diameter of the base circle, and $d$ is the diameter of the tracing
  circle.
  If $d$ becomes closer to $D$, the number of revolutions that the tracing circle does
  about its center becomes arbitrarily large for every one revolution around the base
  circle.
  Instead of circles, a meshing profile is used to maintain an even rolling contact
  between the base and tracing circles (for ease of understanding, gears will be used
  from now on).
  \\\\
  The base and tracing gear teeth profiles may be involute, however only if there is a
  greater than 1 difference between the teeth count of the cycloidal and ring gear, ie
  $D - d > 1$.
  This is due to interference issues with standard involute profiles if the difference
  is 1, and according to Jang et al, a comparison of involute and cycloidal profiles at
  this teeth ratio demonstrates that involute profiles have less rigidity and require
  major pressure angle adjustments to function \cite{InvoluteVsCycloidalComparison}.
\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{figures/InvoluteCycloidalExample.png}
  \caption{Involute Profile in Cycloidal Orbit}
  \label{fig:involute_cycloidal_example}
\end{figure}
The general alternative to involute profiles is pinwheel meshing, where one of either the base or tracing gear is a pinwheel, and the other is a conjugate envelope of the pinwheel.
The key advantage of a cycloidal speed reducer is that the plate gear meshes with multiple pins at all points, guaranteeing no backlash in a perfectly manufactured reducer with more evenly distributed load. Furthermore, the even rolling contact between the plate gear and the lobes reduces wear and increases efficiency.

\subsection{Epitrochoidal Case}
In the epitrochoidal-pinwheel case, the outer ring is the pinwheel, and the inner plate
gear is of an epitrochoidal profile.
\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{figures/Instantaneous Centre of Rotation Epitrochoidal Diagram.png}
  \caption{Instantaneous Centre of Rotation Epitrochoidal Diagram \cite{MechanismAndMachineTheoryPaper}}
  \label{fig:epitrochoidal_IC_diagram}
\end{figure}
The tracing circle is referred to as the cycloidal disc, and the base circle is referred to as the pinwheel.
The cycloidal disc is eccentrically driven with a crank (shown as Link 2 above), and allowed to rotate about its own center ($O_C$), such that it can roll around the pinwheel.
\\\\
By keeping following the reference frame of the cycloidal disc, during its eccentric orbit, the rollers would trace its outer edge.
This profile is the epitrochoidal profile, and is the profile that will be used for the cycloidal disc in the cycloidal speed reducer.
In \cite{Pollitt}, it was derived that to maintain a gear ratio equivalent to the number of lobes on the epitrochoidal profile, the number of rollers must be one greater than the number of lobes on the epitrochoidal profile.
The epitrochoid profile is generated by creating an extended epicycloidal curve, from 2 circles with internal tangency, as described in the following section from \cite{CycloidalKinematics}:
\begin{quote}
  "One alternative for producing pin wheel epitrochoid meshing is illustrated in Fig. 3(a), in which circles I and 2 are in internal tangency and have separate radii PI and P, , respectively. Point 1 is the instantaneous center of rotation. When circle I rotates counter-clockwise around the circumference of circle 2 in a pure rolling motion, the eccentric throw r is the distance between point p and the center of circle I, and point p will generate an extended epicycloid path. Similarly, as shown in Fig. 3(b), in the pin wheel hypotrochoid meshing design, point q will generate an extended hypocycloid path. Therefore, the extended epicycloid and extended hypocycloid paths could be the center of the rollers, meaning that the cycloidal wheel profile can be generated by the inner and outer envclope methods. In other words, the cycloidal wheel profile will be produced using the inward equidistance to the extended cpicycloid and the outward equidistance to the extended hypocycloid. Here, the rollers are the same as the pins."
\end{quote}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{figures/ExtendedTrochoidalCurveGeneration.png}
  \caption{Pinwheel Mesh Trochoidal Curve Generation}
  \label{fig:trochoidal_curve_pinwheel_generation}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Hypotrochoidal Case}
In the hypotrochoidal-pinwheel case, the inner ring is the pinwheel, and the outer
plate gear is of a trochoidal profile.The inner pinwheel is fixed, and the outer plate
gear is eccentrically driven with a crank, and allowed to rotate about its own center,
such that it can roll around the pinwheel.
\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{figures/Instantaneous Centre of Rotation Hypotrochoidal Diagram.png}
  \caption{Instantaneous Centre of Rotation Hypotrochoidal Diagram \cite{MechanismAndMachineTheoryPaper}}
  \label{fig:hypotrochoidal_IC_diagram}
\end{figure}
As shown in the previous section, the hypotrochoidal profile can also be generated by using the extended hypocycloidal curve generation method, and the number of rollers must be one less than the number of lobes on the hypotrochoidal profile to maintain a gear ratio equivalent to the number of lobes on the hypotrochoidal profile.

\subsection{Practical Comparisons}
Hypotrochoidal speed reducers are a newer architecture compared to the more mature
epitrochoidal architecture.
The epitrochoidal architecture has been analysed in many papers for its kinematics,
manufacturing tolerances, stress analysis and performance, while the hypotrochoidal
architecture has no well developed supply chain and is still in the realm of research
and development.
\\\\
Despite this, Hwang and Hsieh \cite{CycloidalKinematics} have shown that hypotrochoidal
speed reducers can provide
more favourable meshing characteristics than the equivalent epitrochoidal
configuration.
This provides a potential advantage in reducing local contact loading and improving
the durability of the contacting surfaces.
However, this result should not be interpreted as demonstrating that a complete
hypotrochoidal reducer will necessarily have higher efficiency or service life,
as bearing losses, manufacturing errors, lubrication, structural deformation,
and output mechanism losses must also be considered.
\\\\
The conventional epitrochoidal configuration provides significant practical
advantages in manufacturability.
Its fixed outer member consists of a circular array of identical cylindrical
pins or rollers, while the more complex profile is located on the external
perimeter of the cycloidal disc.
The cycloidal disc profile can therefore be manufactured using processes such
as wire EDM, profile milling, or grinding, while commercially available hardened
rollers or bearing elements may be used for the pinwheel.
In contrast, the hypotrochoidal arrangement places the complex profile on the
inside surface of an outer ring.
This profile remains suitable for wire EDM when manufactured as a through-profile,
but is less accessible to conventional milling and grinding operations.
\\\\
Undercutting and interference are important constraints for both configurations.
For the hypotrochoidal configuration in particular, Hwang and Hsieh derive a
feasible design region that simultaneously avoids undercutting of the
hypotrochoidal profile and interference between adjacent inner pins
\cite{HypotrochoidalNonUndercutting}.
Consequently, the eccentricity, pin pitch radius, pin radius, and pin count
cannot be selected independently.
The preliminary condition $E<R/N$ derived by Shin and Kwon also applies to the
profile-generation geometry and prevents discontinuities in the contact angle
\cite{MechanismAndMachineTheoryPaper}.
The preliminary advantages and disadvantages of each configuration are summarised
in Table~\ref{tab:epi_hypo_comparison}.

\newpage
Based on the current literature, the outer-pinwheel epitrochoidal configuration
is the lower-risk architecture for the first Node v0 reducer because its
manufacturing, tolerance, stress, and load-sharing behaviour are significantly
better characterised.
The inner-pinwheel hypotrochoidal configuration is nevertheless a credible
alternative because published contact-force and curvature analysis indicates
potentially favourable meshing characteristics \cite{CycloidalKinematics}.
Before selecting either architecture, both should therefore be compared at the
same reduction ratio, outer diameter, material, face width, and rated output
torque.
